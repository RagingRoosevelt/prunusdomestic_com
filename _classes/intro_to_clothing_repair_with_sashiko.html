Class Description

In this class you will learn how to decorate and repair your clothes and other textiles. We'll cover

  • tools and resources needed to practice Sashiko
  • general repair principles
  • how to set up a garment repair for success
  • how to continue to develop your craft

Included in the class is everything you need in order to do Sashiko:

  • Sashiko needles
  • Sashiko thread
  • Sashiko grid templates
  • Water-washable markers

Class Offerings

Classes are offered through Skillcraft Institute in Portland, OR.

Books

My top two favorite reference resources for Sashiko are "Essential Sashiko" by Boutique-sha (PQ, BFB) and Susan Briscoe's "Ultimate Sashiko Card Deck" (PQ). I think both serve very well as a reference for not just design ideas, but also how to sew the designs in a way that flows well and is easy to apply.

I believe I've seen Essential Sashiko at all three shops mentioned above. I've only seen Ultimate Sashiko Card Deck at Pioneer Quilts. You can of course find both online.

Needle & Thread

If you need Sashiko supplies, I'd recommend picking up Sashiko needles and thread from one of the Portland-region stores, listed above. I highly recommend using actual Sashiko thread since it holds up very well to the way Sashiko sewing is performed. Sashiko needles are well-matched to the application due to an eye that is sized correctly for Sashiko thread, a diameter that slides pretty easily through even denim, and a shaft size that is relatively uniform along the entire length even when you get to the eye.

Templates

I have published a number of templates online for free if you have access to a 3D printer. I have both decorative and foundational templates. If you live in Multnomah County, there is a 3D printer service. The West Linn library has a similar program for Clackamas County residents.

You can also buy stickable, printable, washable embroidery transfer "paper". I believe this might also be sold as washable stabilizer. Sulky is a well known brand for this product.

Plastic templates are also available to purchase online and in shops. There are several formats and I find the thin ones (less than 1mm or 1/32") to be the ideal format. Some templates are laser cut acrylic and around 3-4mm / 1/8" thick, but I've found those to be rather difficult to mark through.
